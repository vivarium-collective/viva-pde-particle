Background

Question With a single channel whose opening is coupled to calcium (rate αβ(ρ(0) + 1)), does the hybrid co-simulation reproduce the probability densities of calcium given by direct, Monte-Carlo-free solutions of the Fokker–Planck equations? Two regimes: fast diffusion (well mixed, Test 3) and finite diffusion on coarse grids (Tests 4–5), as in Figs 4–6 of the paper.
Hypothesis The hybrid samples the same piecewise-deterministic process the Fokker–Planck equations describe, so its histograms should match them to within sampling noise (reduced χ² ≈ 1). The paper reports L2 differences of 1–3% of the maximum.

Model & approach

Dimensionless model: ∂τρ = d·∂²ₓρ + a·δ(x)·ξ − ρ. The channel opens at αβ(ρ(0) + 1) and closes at α; it starts closed, with ρ = 0. - **Test 3:** α = β = 1, a = 24, d = 10⁴, 3D cube of side 1 (Δx = 0.1, channel at the centre), Δτ = 0.002. - Compared: p(ρ̄) at τ = 30. - Reference: Eq 4, solved in time and checked against its exact steady state. - Ensembles: 10,000 co-sim trials, and the same on native VCell. - **Test 4:** α = 10, β = 1, a = 20/3, d = 1, two cells with Δx = 2, Δτ = 10⁻⁴. - Compared: p(ρᵢ) at τ = 1. - Reference: the S2-Text master equation (Eq T2.1) at Δρ = 2.5, 1.2 and 0.6 × 10⁻³, extrapolated to Δρ → 0. - Ensembles: 12,500 trials. - **Test 5:** α = 20, β = 0.5, a = 20, d = 50, three cells. - Reference: four Δρ, extrapolated as in the paper. - Ensembles: 12,500 trials at Δτ = 10⁻⁴. Densities are 20-bin histograms on [0, ρ_max(i)] (40 bins on [0, 24] for Test 3), compared with the reference averaged over the same bins. Reported: the relative L2 (RMS over bins / reference maximum), the absolute L2, and the reduced χ² against multinomial sampling noise.

Result

Finite diffusion (Tests 4–5): the co-simulation matches the direct Fokker–Planck solutions to within sampling noise in every cell — reduced χ² = 0.54 (Test 4, x₀), 0.80 (Test 4, x₁) and 0.80 / 0.89 / 0.86 (Test 5, x₀/x₁/x₂), all inside [0, 2]. Relative L2 differences (1.0–1.6%) are smaller than the paper's (1.9% / 3.3% for Test 4).

| test | solver, trials | rel. L2 | sampling noise | reduced χ² | paper | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | 3 (d = 10⁴), ρ̄ at τ = 30 | co-sim, 10,000 | 0.58% | 0.30% | 2.48 | abs. L2 0.0137 (ours 0.021) | | 3 | native VCell, 10,000 | 0.58% | 0.30% | 1.82 | | | 4 (d = 1), ρ(x₀) / ρ(x₁) at τ = 1 | co-sim, 12,500 | 1.0% / 1.6% | 1.5% / 1.7% | 0.54 / 0.80 | 1.9% / 3.3% | | 5 (d = 50), ρ(x₀) / ρ(x₁) / ρ(x₂) | co-sim, 12,500 | 0.9% / 1.2% / 1.4% | ≈ 1.4% | 0.80 / 0.89 / 0.86 | 1.3% / 1.3% / 1.5% |

  • Finite diffusion (Tests 4–5): the co-simulation matches the functional Fokker–Planck solutions to within sampling noise in every cell (reduced χ² 0.54–0.89), with smaller differences than the paper reports.
    • Extrapolating the reference to Δρ → 0 matters: it shifts the Test 5 reference by about 4% of its maximum and Test 4's cell 1 by 1.7%. These are the size of the discrepancies the paper attributes to under-resolved Δρ.
  • Fast diffusion (Test 3): the reference itself is exact. Eq 4 at τ = 30 agrees with its closed-form steady state to 0.03%.
    • Both hybrid solvers differ from it by the same 0.58%, about twice the sampling noise. Native VCell's reduced χ² (1.82) sits just under the threshold, and the co-sim's (2.48) just over it.
    • Since the two solvers share the same small bias, it belongs to the spatial hybrid at Δτ = 0.002, not to the co-simulation.
  • Δτ check (2026-10-08; dtau stage, viz/dtau.html): the Test 3 gap is a first-order time-discretization error.

    • 4,000 co-sim trials per Δτ, each sampled at τ = 10, 15, …, 30 (stationary; lag-5 correlation ≈ 0.01). SEs are bootstrapped over trials.

      | Δτ | mean bias | SD bias | reduced χ² | |---|---|---|---| | 0.008 | −0.45% ± 0.03% | +4.4% ± 1.0% | 28.0 | | 0.004 | −0.25% ± 0.03% | +2.1% ± 1.0% | 10.6 | | 0.002 | −0.12% ± 0.03% | +1.9% ± 1.1% | 2.28 | | 0.001 | −0.12% ± 0.03% | +3.6% ± 1.9% | 2.74 |

    • Mean: the fit offset + slope·Δτ gives a slope of −0.51 ± 0.06 (−0.10% at Δτ = 0.002) and an offset of −0.04% ± 0.03%, consistent with zero (reduced χ² 0.71). Native VCell at Δτ = 0.002 (−0.10% ± 0.04%) matches the slope's prediction.

    • SD: only Δτ = 0.008 is clearly off. The others are within about 2σ. The skewed density (∝ e^ρ) makes the SD's sampling error 2–4× the normal-theory value.

    • The reduced χ² stays above 2 down to Δτ = 0.001. With 20,000 samples, the histogram resolves a 0.1% shift of a density that is steep near ρ = a.
